EUDR for coffee roasters: what applies from 30 December 2026

The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR, Regulation (EU) 2023/1115) covers coffee. Since the amendment of December 2025 (Regulation (EU) 2025/2650), the obligations for most roasters are much lighter – but not zero. This guide explains in five minutes what applies to your roastery.

In short
  • Large and medium-sized companies: from 30 December 2026. Micro and small companies: from 30 June 2027.
  • Only whoever brings the coffee into the EU first files the due diligence statement – usually the importer.
  • If you buy from an importer in the EU, you keep the reference number of its due diligence statement.
  • The coffee must come from land that was not deforested after 31 December 2020.

1. Deadlines

WhoApplies from
Large and medium-sized companies30 December 2026
Micro and small companies30 June 2027

In May 2026 the European Commission confirmed there will be no further delay. Soluble coffee is added later (from 30 December 2027).

2. Which role does your roastery have?

Your obligations depend on where you sit in the supply chain – not on the fact that you roast coffee.

You buy green coffee …Your roleWhat you must do
directly at origin and bring it into the EU yourselfOperatorFull due diligence: geolocation of every plot, risk assessment, due diligence statement in the EU information system before import.
from an importer in the EUFirst downstream operatorNo statement of your own. You collect and keep the reference number of your importer's due diligence statement and record your customers and suppliers.
from a trader or another roaster that is already downstreamFurther downstreamNo reference numbers; you keep information on your direct business partners.

Large downstream companies (non-SMEs) must also register in the EU information system. Many roasters do both – direct trade for some lots and importers for the rest. Then each lot follows its own role.

3. Why it still lands on your desk

Even when your importer files the statement, customers, retailers and auditors increasingly ask the roaster where the coffee comes from and whether the land is deforestation-free. With reference numbers in order and plots you can check yourself, you answer in minutes instead of days – and spot problems before a lot goes into production.

4. Checklist for your roastery

  1. Clarify your role per supplier: direct import (operator) or purchase from an EU importer (downstream)?
  2. Collect reference numbers: file the reference number of your importer's statement for every delivery – ideally per lot.
  3. Prepare direct trade: for lots you import yourself, ask your producers for the geolocation of every plot (point or polygon).
  4. Check the plots: use satellite data to see whether there was forest loss after 31 December 2020.
  5. Keep the records: reference numbers and customer and supplier details for five years.

Frequently asked questions

Do small roasters need to act now?

For micro and small companies the EUDR applies from 30 June 2027. Importers often need the data earlier, though, and many customers already ask. It pays to start collecting reference numbers now.

What is the reference number?

Every due diligence statement gets a reference number in the EU information system. Your importer passes it on with the delivery; you keep it and can show it to the authority on request.

Are certifications like Fairtrade or Rainforest Alliance enough?

Certifications can help with the risk assessment but replace neither the geolocation nor the due diligence statement.
